What you're specifically after — all four countries
🍽️
Cuisine
🇰🇬 KyrgyzstanBeshbarmak — boiled lamb on flat pasta eaten with hands. Manty dumplings, lagman noodle soup, shorpo broth, and kumys at every nomad camp
🇹🇯 TajikistanQurutob — the national dish: flatbread soaked in liquid qurt (dried sour milk) with herbs and onion. Shir chai — pink salty tea. Mountain apricots straight off the tree. Mastoba soup
🇺🇿 UzbekistanPlov — the national obsession. Every city's version is a different dish (Fergana, Samarkand, and Tashkent plov are all distinct). Samsa from tandoor, shashlik over charcoal, non bread, and tea houses on every corner
🇰🇿 KazakhstanBeshbarmak with horse meat and fat-tail sheep. Kazy — smoked horse sausage, an acquired taste absolutely worth acquiring. Baursak (fried bread). Shubat — fermented camel milk, fizzy and sour
🐎
Horseback Riding
🇰🇬 Kyrgyzstan — the heartlandSong Kul, Issyk-Kul shore, Karakol Tian Shan treks. Horse culture is the fabric of daily life here. Arrange a ride at almost any guesthouse
🇹🇯 TajikistanHorse and donkey access into the Fann Mountains and Alauddin Lakes — no other sensible route in. Wakhan Valley rides along the Afghan river border
🇺🇿 UzbekistanLess of a riding destination but buzkashi — traditional horseback polo played with a goat carcass — happens at festivals. Ask in Samarkand and Fergana Valley. Worth witnessing
🇰🇿 KazakhstanSteppe riding in the Almaty region, kokpar (horseback tug-of-war with a goat carcass), and nomadic horse games. The steppe is made for riding — flat, endless, and open
🐑
Wildlife
🇰🇬 KyrgyzstanMarmots everywhere above 2,500m — they whistle at you from burrows. Trained golden eagles at Song Kul. Wild snow leopard in the Tian Shan (rare but real). Brown bear
🇹🇯 TajikistanMarco Polo sheep on the Pamir — world's largest wild sheep, horns up to 1.9m. Ibex in the Fann Mountains at dusk. Wild yaks near Karakul Lake. Snow leopard in the high Pamir
🇺🇿 UzbekistanSaiga antelope on the eastern steppes — critically endangered, herds still visible. Monitor lizards in the Kyzylkum desert near the Aral. Ferghana Valley songbirds and raptors
🇰🇿 KazakhstanSaiga antelope in enormous steppe herds — one of the great wildlife spectacles of Asia. Bactrian camels near the Aral Sea. Steppe eagles overhead constantly. Flamingos at steppe salt lakes
🌄
What each country is famous for
🇰🇬 KyrgyzstanNomadic culture still very much alive. Yurt stays, eagle hunters, horse games, and mountains that go on forever. The least touristy country on the route by a wide margin
🇹🇯 TajikistanThe Pamir Highway — one of the world's great road trips. Extreme hospitality from people who have almost nothing. Wakhan Corridor with Afghanistan across the river. Fann Mountains turquoise lakes
🇺🇿 UzbekistanThe Silk Road. Samarkand, Bukhara, and Khiva are three of the most architecturally extraordinary cities on earth. Islamic tilework, covered bazaars running since the medieval period, and plov as a civic religion
🇰🇿 KazakhstanVast emptiness — steppe that goes to the horizon in every direction. The Aral Sea catastrophe. Charyn Canyon. Soviet space program legacy. And Almaty backed up against mountains that look impossible from the city

Pamir Highway window
The M41 is only reliably open mid-June to mid-September — outside this the high passes can close with snow with no warning
Ak-Baital Pass at 4,655m can get snow any month of the year — always carry extra layers in the jeep
Temperature ranges
Kyrgyzstan mountains: 15–25°C days, can drop below 0°C at Song Kul and Pamir at night even in July
Uzbekistan cities: 35–42°C in July–August. Move in the mornings, rest midday
Kazakhstan (Almaty): Warm and pleasant in summer, cooler in the mountains above the city

👗 How to Dress
General rule across all four countries
This is a conservative Muslim region. Modest dress is respected and expected — not legally required everywhere but it matters socially and you will be treated better for it
Shoulders and knees should be covered in bazaars, guesthouses, and anywhere outside city centres and tourist areas
For women
Loose trousers or long skirts that cover the knee — lightweight cotton works well in summer heat
Tops that cover shoulders — bring a light scarf or shawl for mosques and conservative areas. In Uzbekistan this matters more than in Kyrgyzstan
In Kyrgyzstan with nomad families: dress modestly but people are relaxed. In Uzbekistan old cities: slightly more conservative. Tajikistan rural: most conservative
You do not need to cover your hair outside of mosques, but have a scarf accessible
For men
Long trousers in mosques, religious sites, and rural areas. Shorts are fine in Bishkek and Almaty city centres
T-shirts are fine generally — a collared shirt gets you noticeably warmer receptions in Uzbekistan
In the mountains
Dress rules relax completely in the Pamir, Tian Shan, and Fann Mountains — practical trekking gear is normal and expected
Nomad families at Song Kul will appreciate you dressing comfortably for riding and outdoor life — no modesty pressure here
At mosques and shrines
Women: cover hair with a scarf, cover shoulders and legs. Most sites have scarves available to borrow at the entrance
Men: remove shoes, cover shoulders, avoid shorts. Long trousers required at all active mosques
Sulaiman-Too in Osh and the Shah-i-Zinda in Samarkand are active religious sites — dress accordingly

💊 Medical Kit
Diamox (acetazolamide) 250mgprescriptionAltitude sickness prevention. Take from the evening before crossing high Pamir passes. Ask your doctor — not suitable for everyone. Penicillin allergy contraindicated
Dexamethasone 4mgprescriptionEmergency treatment for severe acute mountain sickness — DO NOT use preventatively. Only if someone can't descend. Ask your doctor
Ciprofloxacin 500mgprescriptionBroad-spectrum antibiotic for severe traveller's diarrhea. Central Asian water and food is a common culprit. Ask your doctor for a course
Tinidazole or Metronidazole (Flagyl)prescriptionFor giardia — a parasite common in mountain water in Central Asia even when purified poorly. Symptoms can appear weeks after. Ask your doctor
Ibuprofen 400mg × 20 tabletsessentialHeadaches are the first symptom of altitude sickness. Also for muscle pain after horse treks and long hiking days
Paracetamol 500mg × 20 tabletsessentialFever, headaches, general pain. Safe to take with Diamox. Alternate with ibuprofen for stronger effect
Imodium (loperamide) × 12 tabletsessentialStomach upset and traveller's diarrhea. Critical for long jeep days when there are no toilet stops. Essential in Central Asia
Oral rehydration salts × 10 sachetsessentialFor any stomach bug and general hydration in heat. Electrolyte sachets for Uzbekistan 38°C days and high altitude dehydration
Antacid (Rennie / Gaviscon) × 10essentialNew cuisine, heavy meat dishes, and oily Uzbek food can cause acid reflux. Have these ready
Antihistamine (cetirizine) × 14 tabletsessentialAllergies, insect bites, dust reactions. Pamir road = 4 days of continuous dust inhalation
Hydrocortisone cream 1%essentialInsect bites, rashes, mild allergic reactions. Mountain bugs and mosquitoes around Issyk-Kul bite relentlessly
Melatonin 0.5–5mgnice to haveJet lag on arrival — Almaty is +3h from Iceland in summer. Helps reset sleep schedule in first 2–3 nights
Compeed blister plasters × 8essentialApply at first sign of hot spot, before the blister forms. Horse trekking and mountain days will find your weak spots
Moleskin paddingessentialCut to shape and apply to hot spots before they blister. Better than Compeed as a preventative measure
Antiseptic wipes + Betadine liquidessentialClean cuts and grazes immediately. Infection risk is higher in rural areas with limited medical access
Bandages, gauze + medical tapeessentialFor larger wounds. Medical care in rural Tajikistan and Kyrgyzstan is very limited — be able to stabilise a wound yourself
Steri-strips (butterfly closures)nice to haveClose deep cuts without stitches. Better than bandages for wounds that need to stay closed. Pack 6–8 strips
ThermometeressentialDistinguish altitude headache from actual fever. If temp is above 38.5°C at altitude, descend — do not wait
Personal prescription medicationsprescriptionBring full 32-day supply plus 1 week extra. Specific medications unavailable in rural Central Asia. Carry in original packaging with prescription

  • Daily budget: $35–55 USD/person — dorms, street food, cheapest transport. Not cheap, not expensive
  • Bishkek → Osh cheapest option: fly for ~$30 USD. Bus takes up to 8 hours. Worth flying to save time
  • Tajikistan e-visa: $50 + $20 GBAO permit = $70 total. GBAO permit required for Pamir Highway. Apply before arriving in Osh — no printing facilities after that
  • Khorog → Dushanbe: marshrutka up to 16 hours, leaves early morning. Or fly on a small aircraft (see the mountains from above)
  • Osh Guesthouse is the main hub for organising Pamir jeep trips and finding other backpackers to share with
  • Caravanistan Forum — best online resource for finding jeep partners ahead of time
  • No internet whatsoever on the Pamir Highway until Dushanbe — tell people you will be gone for a week
  • Song Kul: organise through CBT or Shepherd's Life in Kochkor. More people = cheaper per person
  • Uzbekistan SIM cards only available in Tashkent — get one before leaving the capital

Against the Compass · Joan Torres
Travel to Central Asia — Which Country Should You Visit?
  • Kyrgyzstan = best overall. Cheapest, best tourist infrastructure, lush mountains, nomads everywhere. Recommended first choice
  • Tajikistan = wilder and more remote than KG. Pamir Highway is the highlight. More expensive due to 4WD requirement everywhere. Great for off-the-beaten-path
  • Uzbekistan = most tourist-friendly, most accessible. Silk Road cities are extraordinary but summer (40°C+) is genuinely brutal in cities
  • Kazakhstan = vast, Westernized, weird offbeat attractions. Soviet train across the steppe is an experience in itself
  • Expert tip: if you only have 3 weeks, visit maximum 2 countries, preferably 1. Rushing Central Asia destroys the experience
  • Best season by country: KG + TJ = June–August (mountains). UZ = spring or autumn (cities). Avoid UZ cities in summer
  • From Americas: fly via Istanbul (Turkish Airlines) or Moscow (Aeroflot) — both well connected
  • From Europe: Pegasus Airlines is cheapest, direct flights to Bishkek and Almaty
  • Best guidebooks: Bradt Guides (one per country, most detailed). Lonely Planet good for overview across all 5
  • Best visa/border resource: Caravanistan — updated, community-maintained, essential

Pine Tree Poet · George Callahan
My 30-Day Central Asia Road Trip Itinerary
  • 14 days Kyrgyzstan, 10 days Tajikistan, 7 days Uzbekistan — and KG still felt rushed
  • Hiring a driver for KG and TJ is the move — George used Musa (KG) and his brother (TJ), arranged through the same contact. Driver doubles as translator, fixer, and cultural guide
  • Eagle hunting show in Bokonbaevo — authentic, few tourists, one of the top highlights. Your driver needs to know to arrange it
  • Galloping on horseback at Song Kul to get back to camp — one of the best moments of the entire trip
  • Hiked to 16,000 feet (4,877m) in Tajikistan's Bartang Gorge area — the mountains are genuinely enormous
  • Tea with locals overlooking Afghanistan across the Panj River in the Wakhan — strangers invite you in. Accept it
  • Leave extra days for sickness, car breakdowns, landslides, and over-hospitable locals. 3 breakdowns in 24h due to heat and rugged roads
  • Self-driving in KG is doable — many people stop to help. TJ has military checkpoints and required paperwork, harder
  • Uzbekistan: no car needed. Trains between all major cities work perfectly and cheaply
  • Contact: poet@pinetreepoet.com to get Musa's WhatsApp (recommended driver)

Travels with Essie
Central Asia Itinerary: A Complete 2-Week Guide to the 5 Stans
  • Guided tour (Lupine Travel, all 5 Stans in 2 weeks): ~$2,300/person including accommodation, all meals in UZ, ground transport, guides, visa support
  • Cash only everywhere — USD crisp bills printed after 2013 only. Worn or older notes rejected at Turkmenistan exchanges
  • Non bread (Uzbekistan) is sacred — never place upside down or drop on the floor. It is deeply respected across the region
  • Food tip from experience: avoid chicken, stick to red meats. Chicken caused food poisoning on this trip
  • Rumi Hotel, Dushanbe: their favourite hotel on the entire 5-Stans trip — recommended for your Dushanbe night
  • Border crossings are long and hot — carry toilet paper, wet wipes, and snacks in your day pack for every crossing
  • Bring $1 and $5 USD bills for small transactions in Uzbekistan. Public toilets cost ~5,000 som
