Must-dos by country
🇰🇿Kazakhstan
8 must-dos
  • Aral Sea ship graveyard, Moynaq — the most emotionally powerful stop on the entire trip. Rusting Soviet boats stranded in desert. Total silence. You will feel this one for a long time
  • Charyn Canyon — day trip from Almaty. 300m deep, 90km long, carved rock formations that look genuinely alien. The Central Asian Grand Canyon
  • Big Almaty Lake — turquoise alpine lake at 2,500m, 30km from the city. The color is impossible. Combine with Charyn in one driver day
  • Shymbulak gondola — cable car to 2,260m above Almaty for summer wildflower meadows and views over the entire basin
  • Medeu skating rink — walk down from Shymbulak. Site of 126 world speed skating records. Enormous rink in a mountain bowl
  • Green Bazaar, Almaty — final shopping day. Dried apricots, walnuts, spices, and pomegranate to bring home from the trip
  • Try kazy — smoked horse sausage, the distinctive Kazakh dish. An acquired taste absolutely worth acquiring at least once
  • Stand somewhere in the steppe — find a spot with nothing visible in any direction. Kazakhstan's real gift. Nothing quite like the scale of it
🇰🇬Kyrgyzstan
11 must-dos
  • Song Kul Lake — 2 nights at 3,016m with nomad family. Zero signal, zero electricity. The best thing on this trip
  • Tian Shan horse trek — multi-day above 3,200m from Karakol. Arrange independently through your guesthouse
  • Issyk-Kul southern shore — yurt camp stay, horseback along the steppe, hot springs at Ak Bulak
  • Altyn Arashan hot springs — ride or hike up the valley, soak in outdoor thermal pools at 2,800m overnight
  • Eagle hunting, Bokonbaevo — authentic demonstration, few tourists. Only accessible through a driver who knows the family
  • Ala-Archa gorge — day hike from Bishkek to glacier viewpoints at 3,400m. 40km from the city
  • Sulaiman-Too, Osh — UNESCO sacred mountain. Climb for panoramic views over the city and the Fergana Valley
  • Jayma Bazaar, Osh — 2,000-year-old covered market. No tourist section, just real life happening around you
  • Jeti-Oguz — Seven Bulls red rock formations and Valley of Flowers near Karakol
  • Drink kumys — fermented mare's milk with a nomad family at Song Kul or any yurt camp. Non-negotiable
  • Eat beshbarmak — boiled lamb on flat pasta, eaten with hands. The national dish at its most authentic in a homestay
🇹🇯Tajikistan
11 must-dos
  • Pamir Highway (M41) — full 4-day route Osh to Khorog. One of the greatest road trips on earth. Non-negotiable
  • Ak-Baital Pass — 4,655m. The highest point of the journey. Standing here is something you will not forget
  • Karakul Lake — volcanic crater lake at 3,914m with 7,000m peaks behind it. Surreal blue in the brown desert
  • Wakhan Corridor — stand at the Panj River with Afghanistan 30m across the water. Wave to people on the other side
  • Murgab overnight — the most remote town on the route. 3,600m on the plateau. Nothing around for miles
  • Khorog border walk — walk the riverbank with Afghanistan visible across the Panj. Khorog Botanical Garden
  • Alauddin Lakes, Fann Mountains — turquoise glacial lakes at altitude. Each one a different shade. Among the most beautiful things on this trip
  • Iskanderkul Lake — Alexander the Great's lake. A still emerald reservoir at 2,195m. Brief stop or overnight
  • Bibi Fatima hot springs — sacred hot spring in the Wakhan Valley. Stop on the Pamir route
  • Tea with a stranger — someone will invite you in. Accept every time. The hospitality here is the real highlight of Tajikistan
  • Eat qurutob — flatbread soaked in sour liquid qurt with herbs and onion. The national dish. Try it in Dushanbe
🇺🇿Uzbekistan
11 must-dos
  • Registan at night, Samarkand — visit in daylight once, then return at night when lit up. The second visit is the one you remember forever
  • Shah-i-Zinda, Samarkand — narrow street of 14th century blue-tiled mausoleums. Go at dusk with fewer people
  • Cooking class, Samarkand — make plov, samsa, shashlik in a family kitchen. Best €12 spent on the entire trip
  • Bukhara old city after 9pm — when all day tourists have gone. Empty alleys, lantern light, genuinely ancient feeling
  • Lyab-i Hauz, Bukhara — evening tea around the pool with locals and travelers. One of the great travel evenings in Central Asia
  • Kalon Minaret, Bukhara — 12th century tower that Genghis Khan reportedly spared. The only thing he ever spared
  • Khiva at dawn — arrive before any tour groups. The empty walled city in early morning light is the best photography moment of the trip
  • Islam Khoja Minaret, Khiva — climb for 360° views over the entire walled city at sunset
  • Tashkent metro — buy a ticket and ride the full line just to see the Soviet tile murals and chandeliers at each station
  • Plov Centre, Tashkent — arrive at 8am sharp. The national dish at its absolute best. Sells out by noon without exception
  • Siab Bazaar, Samarkand — spices, dried fruits, fresh non bread. The bread is sacred: never place upside down, never drop it

🎒 What to Bring
Clothing — the key is layers
Base layer (merino wool) — warm at altitude, doesn't smell after days without washing
Fleece mid-layer — essential for Song Kul, Pamir, and Fann Mountains nights
Windproof/waterproof shell — mountain weather changes fast on the Pamir
Light cotton shirts and trousers — for Uzbekistan cities and hot days
Trail runners — one pair covers everything. Add lightweight sandals for yurt camps
Warm hat and gloves — mandatory for Song Kul and Pamir nights in any month
Gear
Sleeping bag liner — guesthouses provide blankets but quality varies wildly
Headlamp — yurt camps and Pamir homestays often have no electricity
Water purification — Steripen + Aquatabs backup — tap water unreliable outside cities
Buff / neck gaiter × 2 — Pamir road = 4 days of serious dust
Sunscreen SPF 50+ and lip balm — UV at 4,000m is brutal, burns happen fast
Portable battery pack 20,000mAh — power cuts common in rural areas
Dry bags — protect electronics and documents on the rough Pamir road
Money
Bring USD $600+ cash per person — ATMs nonexistent in the Pamir and rural areas
Exchange to local currency in city bazaars — always better rates than banks or airports
Money belt — worn under clothes at border crossings and busy bazaars
👗 How to Dress
General rule across all four countries
This is a conservative Muslim region. Modest dress is respected and expected — not legally required everywhere but it matters socially
Shoulders and knees covered in bazaars, guesthouses, and anywhere outside city centres
For women
Loose trousers or long skirts covering the knee — lightweight cotton works well in summer heat
Tops that cover shoulders — bring a light scarf for mosques and conservative areas
Kyrgyzstan with nomads: relaxed. Uzbekistan old cities: slightly more conservative. Tajikistan rural: most conservative
For men
Long trousers in mosques, religious sites, and rural areas. Shorts fine in Bishkek and Almaty city centres
A collared shirt gets noticeably warmer receptions in Uzbekistan
In the mountains
Dress rules relax completely in the Pamir, Tian Shan, and Fann Mountains — practical trekking gear is normal
At mosques and shrines
Women: cover hair, shoulders, and legs. Most sites have scarves to borrow at the entrance
Men: remove shoes, cover shoulders, long trousers required
Sulaiman-Too in Osh and Shah-i-Zinda in Samarkand are active religious sites — dress accordingly

The Complete Guide to Travel in Uzbekistan 2026
  • USA citizens: fully visa-free from January 1, 2026. EU, Australia, NZ also visa-free 30 days. Overstay fine: $2,000 USD + possible week+ detention
  • Tashkent airport taxi: walk LEFT past the parking lot to find drivers returning to the city. Max 25,000 som (~$2-3). Do NOT pay the waiting drivers at the gate. Or use Yandex Go (~$3)

  • Bread is sacred: never flip upside down, never drop, never throw away

The Complete Tajikistan Travel Guide 2026
  • Get the e-visa even if your passport is on the visa-free list — land border guards at Kyzylart have turned back visa-free travelers. The e-visa removes all doubt. $30 for 60 days. Add GBAO permit for $20 at the same time
  • If staying over 10 days visa-free: register at OVIR within 10 days. ~210 TJS (~$20). Your guesthouse can do it for 250-300 TJS
  • Best time: September–October — hiking still open, cooler, fresh fruit everywhere, fewer tourists. June–September for Pamir Highway snow-free access
  • Homestays on Pamir Highway: ~$12-13/night including dinner + breakfast. Dushanbe hostel dorm: ~$4-5/night. Meals: ~$2 each
  • Drones are allowed in Tajikistan for recreational use — no permit required
  • You speak Persian — your language advantage is strongest in Tajikistan. Tajik is a dialect of Farsi. Rural Fann Mountains families barely speak Russian. Your Farsi works in the deepest corners of the country

A Guide to Traveling in Tajikistan — Route, Tips, 3-Week Itinerary
  • GBAO permit at OVIR Dushanbe: only 20 Somoni (~$2) — far cheaper than via e-visa route. Also available in Khujand and Panjakent
  • Police corruption scams: officers invent visa problems or fake entrance fees. There is never a problem with your visa. Pretend you don't speak Russian or English. Waste their time until they let you go
  • Never pay a bribe — it encourages further extortion of both tourists and locals
  • Shared taxi Dushanbe → Khorog: ~$30 USD, ~12 hours. Leave by 7am from near the market
  • Green House Hostel, Dushanbe: comfortable, big kitchen, great common room. Recommended base
  • Fann Mountains: recommend 7 Lakes (Haft Kul) trek over Iskanderkul — more villages, more local life. Iskanderkul is beautiful but packed with domestic tourists
  • Penjakent → Samarkand border: easy, quick, and the natural route between Fann Mountains and Uzbekistan
  • Khujand: spend at least 2 nights. Oldest city in Central Asia (2,500 years), traditional bazaar, authentic, almost no tourists

The Best Guide to Travel in Kazakhstan 2026
  • Exchange rate 2026: $1 = 460 KZT, €1 = 494 KZT
  • Wild camping permitted anywhere. Bears, wolves, and ticks in mountains. Snakes and scorpions in desert. Be prepared in remote areas
  • Drones allowed with a license from the Aviation Committee in Nur-Sultan. Without one: fine, detention, or confiscation
  • Train system is excellent. Book on tickets.kz and select your bunk in advance. Overnight trains include sheets and pillow. Bring food — dining car is expensive
  • 2GIS app: more accurate than Maps.me for navigating Almaty, Shymkent, and Astana. iOverlander: essential for road trips — camping sites, mechanics, fuel stations
  • Shymbulak: world-class ski resort above Almaty, open year-round. Summer hiking and cable car worth it even without skiing
  • Kumys (fermented mare's milk) is the national drink. Kazakhstan is the world's 7th biggest vodka consumer
  • Traffic police road trips: stay calm, don't pay bribes, pretend you don't speak Russian. They will give up
